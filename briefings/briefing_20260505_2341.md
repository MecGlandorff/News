**Status:** New | **Confidence:** Medium | **Source agreement:** Single Source | **Dispute:** None

### Why it matters
Nissan plans to close one production line in the United Kingdom and cut 900 jobs in Europe, according to the BBC. The company also says it is considering working with a third party to make fuller use of its Sunderland plant. The announcement points to the familiar pressure on European carmaking: weak demand, costly electrification, excess capacity and the awkward economics of factories built for volumes that no longer look assured. The third-party option is the revealing part: Nissan appears to be looking for asset utilisation without bearing the full burden alone. That may preserve some industrial activity, but it also signals that legacy producers are increasingly treating plant capacity as a problem to be managed rather than a strategic prize.
