### Why it matters

Restoration may still leave Iran’s filtered national internet architecture stronger.

### Why it matters
Frisian appears more literate, not merely more sentimental. A new provincial “Taalatlas” reported by NOS says a growing share of people in Friesland say they can read and write Frisian well. In 2025, 58% said they could read Frisian well or very well, up from 46% in 2007. The share claiming very good writing ability doubled to about 19%. Younger Frisians, especially those aged 18-29, more often report good reading ability, possibly helped by social-media use. Education policy is now moving in the same direction: Frisian must play a larger role across lessons, and every secondary pupil should be able to sit a Frisian exam from 2030.

Emergency services were called to the Drierivierenlaan around 6pm after a carbon-monoxide alarm went off in one apartment. Firefighters then measured the building and confirmed the presence of CO, according to regional broadcaster Rijnmond as cited by NOS. Two residents were taken to hospital; one had become unwell. The incident is local, but the risk is a recurring one in Dutch housing: NOS notes that acute CO poisoning kills roughly ten to fifteen people a year in the Netherlands.
