## 6. NEW STORY Pieter Baan waiting times

**Status:** Cooling | **Confidence:** Medium | **Source agreement:** Single Source | **Dispute:** None

### Why it matters
NOS reports that waiting times for psychiatric observation at the Pieter Baan Centrum have fallen sharply. Suspects previously waited up to nine months for admission; now the wait is three to four months, or about 12 to 15 weeks from registration. The PBC examines roughly 200 to 220 suspects a year, often in serious cases such as murder, manslaughter or rape, and advises courts on criminal responsibility and possible treatment, including tbs. The improvement matters because long waits delay trials and prolong uncertainty for victims, defendants and courts. The earlier backlog reflected more requests, temporary closures during bathroom renovations and shortages of psychologists and psychiatrists. The PBC cautions that waits cannot realistically fall much below ten weeks because files must be complete and experts need preparation time. There is also a rebound risk: courts and prosecutors had adjusted to long waits; if admission becomes easier, they may request more intensive observations again.

### What to watch
- Do observation requests rise now that waiting times have fallen?
- Can staffing levels sustain the shorter queue?
- Will delayed criminal cases now move faster through court?
