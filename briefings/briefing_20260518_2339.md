**Status:** Developing | **Confidence:** High | **Source agreement:** Broad | **Dispute:** None

**What changed today:** Two lawyers said they are willing to defend Ridouan Taghi in the Marengo appeal if granted enough time to study the vast dossier, while prosecutors argued the case should no longer wait.

### Why it matters
The Marengo appeal has acquired a possible exit from its representation deadlock, but not a clean one. Multiple Dutch outlets report that lawyers Anique Slijters and Ronald van der Horst are willing to assist Ridouan Taghi, on condition they receive enough time to read the full case file. Het Parool puts that file at 90,000 pages. NU.nl reports the prosecution arguing during an “exotic” hearing that the time for waiting is over and that lack of counsel need not necessarily block a fair trial. The court therefore faces a procedural trade-off: granting extensive reading time protects appeal legitimacy but risks another long delay; pushing ahead preserves momentum but invites human-rights and fairness challenges. Taghi’s centrality to the Marengo case makes the issue unusually consequential. A trial of this scale cannot easily appear both efficient and under-defended. Today’s change is that representation may be possible—but only if the court pays for it in time.

### What to watch
- How much preparation time will the court grant the two lawyers?
- Will Taghi accept them as counsel?
- Does the prosecution challenge any delay as excessive?
