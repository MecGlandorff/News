## 2. NEW STORY The Hague home schooling
_Dutch Politics — importance 4.0 — 3 sources — latest reported 2026-05-25 16:27 UTC_

**Status:** New | **Confidence:** High | **Source agreement:** Broad | **Dispute:** None

**What changed today:** First detected today.

### Why it matters
The Hague will stop granting religious or philosophical exemptions from compulsory schooling to roughly 100 children and expects them back in class after the summer. The move follows a Dutch Supreme Court ruling that parents can claim such an exemption only if no public school is available nearby. For primary pupils that means within six kilometres; for secondary pupils, within twenty. In a city such as The Hague, the municipality argues, that condition is not met. The case matters because home-schooled children under this exemption have largely been outside inspection: officials do not know in all cases whether they receive education, or what it contains. The city is likely to become a test case for other municipalities. The political fault line is between parental conscience and the state’s duty to guarantee education. Enforcement will be the hard part: refusing new exemptions is simple; compelling attendance after years outside school is not.

### What to watch
- Do parents challenge The Hague’s interpretation in court?
- Will other municipalities adopt the same policy before national legislation changes?
- How will attendance be enforced if children do not appear after the summer?
